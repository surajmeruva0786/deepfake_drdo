Project: Multi-Modal Audio-Visual Deepfake Detection Framework

DATASETS UTILIZED
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Visual Baseline Dataset (PGCF Training): Celeb-DF-v2
   - Used exclusively for training the visual-only Physical-Guided Consistency Framework (PGCF).
   - Contains high-quality visual manipulations, establishing a strong foundation for spatial artifact detection.

2. Audio & Multi-Modal Dataset (AGCF Training & In-Domain Testing): FakeAVCeleb
   - Used for training and validating the Audio-Guided Consistency Framework (AGCF), as well as evaluating the final Multi-Modal Fusion framework.
   - Contains a diverse mix of authentic videos and multi-modal forgeries (e.g., FaceSwap, Wav2Lip, DeepFaceLab).
   - Note: Meticulously split into an 80/20 train/validation ratio using an Identity-Disjoint strategy to prevent biometric leakage.


1. PHASE 1: The Visual-Only Baseline (Physical-Guided Consistency Framework)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective: To establish a foundational deepfake detection baseline relying exclusively on spatial and visual anomalies within the video domain.
Methodology & Implementation: 
The initial architecture, designated as the Physical-Guided Consistency Framework (PGCF), was constructed to analyze visual frame sequences. The pipeline uniformly sampled 32 frames from each video clip to capture spatial inconsistencies, blending boundaries, and physiological irregularities (such as unnatural blinking or facial distortion). A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) backbone was utilized for feature extraction.
Limitations & Motivation for Evolution:
While the PGCF demonstrated high efficacy in detecting purely visual manipulations (e.g., FaceSwap or DeepFaceLab), it exposed a critical conceptual vulnerability: it was fundamentally blind to Audio-Driven Deepfakes (e.g., Wav2Lip). In audio-driven forgeries, the visual frames often remain authentic while the audio track and lip-sync are maliciously manipulated. Consequently, the visual-only model produced severe False Negatives when encountering high-quality lip-sync deepfakes, proving that a single-modality approach is insufficient for generalized forgery detection.

2. PHASE 2: Introducing the Audio Modality (Audio-Guided Consistency Framework)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective: To develop an independent Audio-Guided Consistency Framework (AGCF) capable of running parallel to the visual stream, specifically targeting spectral and vocal anomalies introduced by synthetic voice generation algorithms.
Architecture Details:
To capture the full spectrum of acoustic manipulation, a dual-stream architecture was engineered:
  a) Spectral Stream: We extracted Log-Mel Spectrograms representing the frequency domain. Utilizing Librosa, the audio was resampled to 16kHz, and spectrograms were computed with 64 mel bins across 100 temporal frames. These (1, 64, 100) tensors were processed through a 2D CNN to detect unnatural spectral frequencies and phase irregularities common in vocoder-generated speech.
  b) Vocal Stream: We analyzed the raw audio waveforms directly in the time domain. 16,000-dimensional arrays (representing 1 second of audio) were processed via 1D CNNs to capture robotic or synthetic timbre artifacts that are often lost during spectrogram conversion.
Temporal Aggregation: 
The high-dimensional feature maps extracted by the dual CNN streams were flattened and concatenated. To capture long-term sequential dependencies in human speech, the aggregated features were fed into a Transformer mechanism (utilizing self-attention to weigh the global context of the audio clip) followed by a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) layer to capture both forward and backward temporal dependencies. The network was optimized using Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss.

3. PHASE 3: Ensuring Evaluation Integrity (Solving Data Leakage)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
During rigorous pipeline audits of the AGCF training loop on the FakeAVCeleb dataset, two critical data-handling flaws were identified and resolved to ensure the scientific integrity of the evaluation metrics:
- 1. Identity-Level Data Leakage (Overfitting via Biometrics): 
  The initial 80/20 dataset split utilized random file-level shuffling. Extensive analysis revealed 130 overlapping human identities between the training and validation sets. Because multiple clips of the same subject existed, the model was implicitly memorizing specific celebrity voices rather than learning generalized deepfake artifacts. 
  Resolution: We completely re-engineered the data loader (`dataset.py`) to implement a strict Identity-Disjoint Split. All identities (e.g., 'id00078') were parsed, grouped, and split at the subject level. This guaranteed zero identity overlap, forcing the model to perform true zero-shot evaluation on entirely unseen speakers.
- 2. Evaluation Metric Inflation via Cartesian Merges: 
  Real videos in the dataset often shared generic filenames (e.g., `00005.mp4` existed across dozens of different subject folders). When merging audio and video prediction CSVs using solely the filename as the key, a Pandas Cartesian product explosion occurred, falsely duplicating the "Real" predictions and artificially inflating accuracy metrics.
  Resolution: We updated the evaluation scripts (`eval.py`) to construct globally unique identifiers by prepending the unique parent directory ID to the filename (e.g., `id00078_00005.mp4`).

Unbiased AGCF (Audio-Only) Metrics Post-Correction (Evaluated on Unseen Identities):
  - Accuracy:  0.7879
  - AUC:       0.8836
  - Precision: 0.8590
  - Recall:    0.6837
  - F1 Score:  0.7614

4. PHASE 4: The Multi-Modal Late Fusion Framework (Video + Audio)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective: To synthesize the spatial awareness of the PGCF (Video) with the temporal-audio awareness of the AGCF (Audio) to create an impenetrable, multi-modal deepfake classifier.
Methodology: 
Rather than relying on early feature concatenation, we implemented a Late Fusion architecture, allowing both the Video and Audio networks to specialize in their respective domains before merging their final predictive probabilities. We evaluated two distinct logic gates:
- Soft Fusion (Product Strategy): The element-wise multiplication of the probability scores from both models, creating a continuous confidence threshold.
- Hard Fusion (AND Gate Logic): A strict binary consensus mechanism requiring both the visual and audio streams to independently classify the video as "Fake" to trigger a positive detection.
Results & Synergistic Effects:
Combining the modalities drastically reduced False Positives. The audio stream successfully caught manipulated audio in visually authentic videos, while the video stream rejected low-quality real audio that the AGCF misclassified. 
The Hard AND Logic achieved state-of-the-art peak performance metrics on our test set:
  - Accuracy:  0.9862
  - Precision: 1.0000 (Achieving a 0% False Positive Rate on Real Videos)
  - Recall:    0.7894
  - F1 Score:  0.8823
Visualization Suite: We developed a comprehensive analytical visualization suite to generate ROC curves, Precision-Recall curves, and Confusion Matrix heatmaps, definitively proving the quantitative superiority of the fused multi-modal approach over isolated single-modality models.

5. PHASE 5: Cross-Dataset Generalization & Robustness Testing
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective: To validate the framework's capacity to generalize to entirely unseen manipulation algorithms and external identities by cross-testing on a foreign dataset.
Dataset: Localized Audio Visual DeepFake Dataset (LAV-DF).
Implementation: 
To bypass network instability and massive archive sizes (23.1GB) during automated dataset acquisition, we designed a resilient, auto-resuming extraction pipeline (`download_and_extract_lavdf.py`). We built a filtration script to systematically parse the downloaded archive and isolate exactly 150 Real and 150 Fake videos, ensuring a perfectly balanced 300-video subset. We engineered a cross-evaluation pipeline (`cross_eval.py`) to execute zero-shot inference on this subset, generating definitive cross-dataset matrices to prove the real-world viability and robustness of our Multi-Modal Late Fusion architecture.
